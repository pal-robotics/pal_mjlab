PAL Kangaroo - Stand-up task
=============================

The stand-up task trains Kangaroo (simple model, 4 DoF per arm) to get back on
its feet from any resting posture: lying face down, on its back or on a side,
sitting, crouching, or already standing. It has to reach the default standing
pose and stay there, without moving violently.

The environment configuration extends the flat velocity task and uses the
Kangaroo model with mesh collisions (``get_kangaroo_fall_robot_cfg``), since
every link can end up on the ground.


Start states
------------

Three episodes in ten start standing in the default pose, so that the policy
learns to stay up, not only to get up. The others start at rest in a state
taken from a library that is generated by simulation when the environment is
created: robots dropped in random orientations while their joints are driven
to random targets, and robots lowered into a squat. States whose hip or ankle
joints rest outside their convex hulls are left out of the library.

During training, pushes set the base velocity every 3 to 6 s, up to 0.5 m/s
horizontally and 0.3 rad/s in rotation.


Observations
------------

**Actor :**

+------------------------------------+-----------+
| Name                               |   Shape   |
+====================================+===========+
| base_ang_vel                       |    (3,)   |
+------------------------------------+-----------+
| joint_pos                          |   (26,)   |
+------------------------------------+-----------+
| joint_vel                          |   (26,)   |
+------------------------------------+-----------+
| actions                            |   (22,)   |
+------------------------------------+-----------+
| imu_projected_gravity              |    (3,)   |
+------------------------------------+-----------+

|

These are the actor observations of the velocity task without the command.
The critic additionally receives the base linear velocity and acceleration,
the foot contacts, the ground force on every body, the base height and the
tilt of the torso. It does not receive the time left in the episode: the
deployment has no end.

.. important::

   Before the first step of an episode, ``actions`` holds the action that
   keeps the robot in the pose it is in,
   ``(joint position - default joint position) / action scale``, and the
   action smoothness rewards count from it. The deployment has to initialize
   the last action the same way.


Actions
-------

Joint position targets, as in the velocity task. The arm joints use the action
scale they would have with actuators of 1 Hz natural frequency instead of
3.5 Hz (12.25 times larger); the actuator gains are unchanged.

-------

|

*Progress* terms cover the stages of getting up: torso upright, weight on the
feet, base at height, standing on the feet only, default pose, at rest.
*Care* terms penalize what a violent motion shows: joint speed beyond 2 rad/s
(0.5 m/s for the leg length), actuator force and force demanded beyond 80 % of
the limit, ground force peaks beyond 1.2 robot weights on a body (1.3 on the
feet), base speed, IMU acceleration more than 3 m/s² away from gravity,
self-contacts above 200 N and jerky actions. *Limits* terms are those of the
velocity task, except the hip and ankle convex hulls (the joint pairs the
real transmission can reach): the distance outside a hull shrunk by 0.05 rad
is penalized linearly, for the measured positions and, with a lower weight,
for the joint targets, which lead the positions out.

The care and hull weights in the table are the initial ones; the curriculum
raises them. At their final weights from the start, the hulls keep the policy
from finding how to get up from lying.


Curriculum
----------

Training starts with a lift: an upward force on the torso of one robot weight
while the base is on the ground, falling to nothing at standing height.
Without it the policy does not learn to get up from lying.

``stand_up_curriculum`` follows the share of episodes that end with the robot
standing and, every 25 iterations:

- lowers the lift by 5 % of the robot's weight while that share is above
  95 %, until it is gone, and raises it again by as much when the share is
  below 90 %;
- then multiplies the care and hull weights by 1.1 while the share is above
  95 % and divides them by 1.1 when it is below 90 %. ``joint_speed``,
  ``ground_force`` and ``base_acceleration`` go up to 50 times their initial
  weight, the hull terms up to 20 times (-20 on the positions, -5 on the
  targets), the other care terms up to 10 times.

The steps are small because the share follows them late: an episode counts
when it ends, some 15 s after the robot got up, and few episodes start fallen.
Larger steps take the lift or the hulls further than the policy can follow,
and it forgets how to get up from lying.

The curriculum state is saved with the checkpoints. The lift is off in play.


Terminations
------------

An episode lasts 20 s (a truncation, not a failure). It ends early, as a
failure, if the robot is down again after it stood for 0.5 s: a body other
than a foot touches the ground or the torso is tilted more than 45°. Play has
neither: the robot keeps going, and gets up again if it falls.


Training and evaluation
-----------------------

.. code-block:: bash

   uv run train Mjlab-StandUp-Flat-Pal-Kangaroo --env.scene.num-envs 4096

   uv run play Mjlab-StandUp-Flat-Pal-Kangaroo --checkpoint-file /path/to/model.pt --num-envs 16

After 2500 iterations (2 hours on an RTX 5060 Ti), the policy stands up in
99 % of 4096 episodes, in about 4 s from lying down:
